# Repair Record 001: Rafoogari (Najibabad / Delhi)

**Type:** living-practice and oral-history record (tradition level, with practitioner testimony). This is not yet a single-mend instance record; it documents the tradition and a master practitioner's account, pending instance-level records with before/during/after documentation and a retained sample.

## Record (Repair Record fields)

- **A. Object and context:** Kashmiri pashmina and *kani* shawls (pashmina / camelid wool). Tradition: *rafoogari*, invisible reweaving practised by hereditary *rafoogars* concentrated in Najibabad and Delhi, India. Atlas link: Invisible / restorative > Rafoogari (India: Najibabad/Delhi).
- **B. Damage (typical):** moth-holes, tears and wear in fine woven shawls.
- **C. Intervention (technique):** rebuilding the cloth by reweaving warp and weft thread by thread, using yarn harvested from the garment's own hems and seams, so that the mend disappears on the face of the fabric. Intent mode: invisible-restorative. Skill register: masterly. Maker: Rizwan Khan (rafoogar), recorded with consent.
- **D. Result:** detectability at or near 0 (invisible on the face of the cloth).
- **E. Physical specimen:** not yet collected (to be added in a future instance record).
- **F. Rights and governance:** the practitioner consented to recording, publication, and machine-processing. Attribution: Rizwan Khan. Benefit-sharing: intended (see note). The full interview transcript is held by the author and is withheld from open release here pending the practitioner's agreement on the scope of open deposit and a benefit-sharing arrangement.
- **G. Record provenance:** recorded by Saba Alvi, September 2026; video interview (Hindi), shared as an unlisted recording at https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1FNUAzyK7XI ; transcribed via speech-to-text and reconciled against a human translation. Capture methods: audiovisual, written.

## Vitality and threat (from the interview)

The practitioner locates the threat in lost livelihood rather than lost records: the work pays too little to retain artisans, and private practice is unviable unless artisans are first retained and compensated. This record is deposited on the principle that documentation is necessary but not sufficient, and must travel with fair pay, attribution, and demand-side support.
